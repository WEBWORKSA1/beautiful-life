Big trips are wonderful but rare. Micro-adventures — short, local, low-cost experiences that break routine — can bring novelty and awe into ordinary weeks. Researchers who study awe describe it as the feeling of encountering something vast that changes how you see the world, and it can be found far closer to home than you might think.

Why novelty matters

Routine makes time blur: weeks that look the same are hard to tell apart in memory. New experiences create distinct memories, which can make life feel richer and fuller. Novelty, shared with others, also strengthens relationships.

25 micro-adventure ideas

Outdoors

  • Watch the sunrise from the highest point in your town.
  • Sleep under the stars in the garden or at a nearby campsite.
  • Walk a local trail you've never tried.
  • Go wild swimming at a safe, supervised spot.
  • Follow a river or stream as far as you can in two hours.
  • Go stargazing away from city lights.
  • Try foraging with a qualified guide.

In town

  • Take a bus to the end of the line and explore.
  • Eat a cuisine you've never tried.
  • Visit a museum and focus on just one room.
  • Be a tourist in your own city for a day.
  • Attend a free lecture or community event.
  • Find the oldest building in your area and learn its story.

Creative and social

  • Take a one-off class: pottery, dance, climbing.
  • Host a themed dinner with friends.
  • Start a "first Friday" tradition of doing something new each month.
  • Photograph your neighbourhood from a new perspective.
  • Learn to cook a dish from your family's heritage.
  • Volunteer somewhere unfamiliar.
  • Write a letter to your future self and seal it for a year.
  • Try an open-mic night — watching or performing.
  • Spend a phone-free day.
  • Plan a picnic in an unusual location.
  • Join a local walking tour.
  • Take a different route to work every day for a week.
