Your breath is the only part of your nervous system you can control directly and instantly. Slowing your breathing — especially lengthening the exhale — is widely used to shift the body toward a calmer state. These three techniques take less than five minutes and can be done anywhere.

1. Box breathing (4-4-4-4)

Breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 4, breathe out for 4, hold for 4. Repeat for four to six rounds. The even rhythm makes it easy to remember and gives an anxious mind something to focus on.

Best for: before a presentation, a difficult conversation or any moment you need composure.

2. 4-7-8 breathing

Breathe in quietly through your nose for 4 counts, hold for 7, then exhale slowly through your mouth for 8. Repeat up to four times. The long exhale makes this a popular choice at bedtime.

Best for: winding down before sleep or after a stressful day.

3. Coherent breathing (5-5)

Breathe in for about 5 seconds and out for about 5 seconds, smoothly and without holds — roughly six breaths per minute. Continue for five minutes or longer.

Best for: a daily practice to build calm over time.

Tips: breathe through your nose where possible, let your belly rather than your shoulders rise, and never force the breath. If you feel light-headed, return to normal breathing.
