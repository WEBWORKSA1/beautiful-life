An emergency fund is money set aside for the unexpected: a job loss, a car repair, an urgent trip home. It isn't exciting, but it is the single financial habit most likely to turn a crisis into an inconvenience. Here's how to build one, step by step.

How much do you need?

A common guideline is three to six months of essential expenses — rent or mortgage, utilities, food, insurance, transport and minimum debt payments. Aim for the higher end if your income is irregular, you're self-employed or you support others. Start smaller, though: even one month of expenses provides real protection.

The four-stage plan

  1. Starter buffer: save a small, fixed amount — enough to cover a typical surprise bill. This stops small emergencies going onto high-interest credit.
  2. One month: build to one month of essential expenses.
  3. Three months: the point where most people say they feel "safe".
  4. Six months: full peace of mind, especially for variable incomes.

Where to keep it

The fund should be safe, separate and accessible. A high-interest savings account at a regulated institution, separate from your everyday account, is a common choice. Separation matters: if the money sits next to your spending money, it tends to get spent.

How to find the money

  • Automate it: set up a transfer on payday so saving happens before spending.
  • Save windfalls: tax refunds, bonuses and gifts can jump-start the fund.
  • Cancel one subscription and redirect the payment.
  • Round up: many banks offer features that round purchases up and save the difference.
  • Sell what you don't use: clearing clutter can fund your first stage.
Define "emergency" in advance. Write down what counts — job loss, medical costs, essential repairs — and what doesn't (sales, holidays). Deciding now protects the fund from future temptation.

After you use it

Using the fund isn't failure; it's the fund doing its job. Pause other goals briefly and rebuild it before resuming.
