Most of us only notice that something is off when it becomes a crisis: burnout, a health scare, a relationship at breaking point. The Wheel of Life is a simple coaching exercise that catches imbalance early. It takes about fifteen minutes and gives you a single picture of your whole life.

How the exercise works

You divide life into a handful of areas — we use eight: Mind, Body, Money, Love, Home, Purpose, Adventure and Growth. You rate your satisfaction with each from 1 to 10, then plot the scores on a circle. The result looks like a wheel, and the question is obvious: how smoothly would this wheel roll?

Step 1: Rate honestly, not ideally

Score how things are today, not how they were last year or how you hope they'll be. A useful rule: a 10 means you would not change anything; a 5 means it's fine but you often think about it; a 2 means it's actively hurting other areas of your life.

Step 2: Look for the shape, not the average

An average of 6 can hide very different lives. Someone with 9s in Money and Purpose and 2s in Body and Love has a very different problem from someone with steady 6s everywhere. Large gaps between your highest and lowest areas usually mean the strong areas are being funded by the weak ones — long hours at work paid for with sleep and relationships, for example.

Step 3: Choose one area — and only one

It's tempting to fix everything. Don't. Choose the area where a two-point improvement would most change how you feel day to day. Often that's the lowest score, but not always: sometimes a modest improvement in Body (better sleep) lifts Mind, Love and Growth at the same time.

The ripple test: for each area, ask "If this went up by two points, how many other areas would improve too?" Choose the area with the most ripples.

Step 4: Define a 30-day experiment

Turn the chosen area into one specific, measurable behaviour you'll try for 30 days. "Improve health" is a wish. "Lights out by 11pm on weeknights" is an experiment. Track it with our free habit tracker.

Step 5: Re-score every month

Repeat the wheel monthly. You'll see whether your experiment worked and spot new imbalances before they turn into problems. Over a year, those twelve snapshots become a remarkably honest diary of your life.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing your wheel with someone else's. The point is your own balance, not a competition.
  • Treating 10 as the goal everywhere. A life of all 10s doesn't exist. Aim for no area below 5 and steady movement upward.
  • Skipping the action step. Insight without an experiment changes nothing.
